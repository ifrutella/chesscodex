The Ware Opening is 1.a4, an irregular first move classified as A00 and also known as the Meadow Hay Opening. It attacks the b5-square and prepares to bring the a1-rook into the game, and it ignores the centre completely.

### Origins

The opening is named after Preston Ware, a U.S. player who often played uncommon openings.

At the 2012 World Blitz Championship Magnus Carlsen played 1.a4 as a little joke against Teimour Radjabov. During the blitz championship two years earlier Radjabov had told him: "Everyone is getting tired. You might as well start with 1.a4 and you can still beat them." The game soon turned into a sort of Four Knights Game, and Carlsen won it. He played 1.a4 again in the blitz portion of Norway Chess 2022 and lost to Wesley So.

### The idea

The b5-square is not essential, and after 1...e5 the bishop on f8 stops the rook from coming out for the moment. With 1...e5 Black also gains space in the centre, a typical aim of most openings. For these reasons the Ware Opening is normally played only by complete newcomers to chess.

An experienced player who uses it will usually answer 1...d5 with 2.d4 and 1...e5 with 2.e4, since a reversed Scandinavian or Englund Gambit would be unsound here. After 1...d5 2.Nf3 the move 2...Nf6 is recommended, because 2...c5 3.e4 dxe4 4.Ne5 gives a reversed Fajarowicz in which the pawn on a4 has some use.

### Main lines

The best-known named lines are:

- 1...e5 2.a5 d5 3.e3 f5 4.a6, the [Ware Gambit](/openings/ware-opening-ware-gambit). White tries to open a file in exchange for a pawn.
- 1...e5 2.h4, the [Crab Variation](/openings/ware-opening-crab-variation). It is generally chosen for its looks: White's pawns extend like a crab's pincers.
- 1...b6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 Nd7, the [Cologne Gambit](/openings/ware-opening-cologne-gambit).
- 1...b5 2.axb5 Bb7, the [Wing Gambit](/openings/ware-opening-wing-gambit).
- 1...a5, the [Symmetric Variation](/openings/ware-opening-symmetric-variation).
- 1...e5 2.Ra3, the [Meadow Hay Trap](/openings/ware-opening-meadow-hay-trap), where Black's bishop can take the rook with 2...Bxa3.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 49.9% of about 991,000 games and White 45.5%, with 4.6% drawn. Under 1400 Black wins 53.4% and White 39.7%, and between 1400 and 1799 it is 53.4% against 41.4%. Between 1800 and 2199 Black leads 48.5% to 47.1%. From 2200 up White is ahead, 49.8% against 44.0%. Only 29 master games are in the database, too few to judge.
