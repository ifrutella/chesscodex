The Tarrasch Trap on this page arises in the [Steinitz Defence](/openings/ruy-lopez-steinitz-defense) of the Ruy Lopez: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 d6 4.d4 Bd7 5.Nc3 Nf6 6.O-O Be7 7.Re1 O-O?. Castling looks natural, but it loses a pawn. The trap is sometimes called the Dresden Trap; Lichess files it as a Berlin Defense line, and the code is C66.

### Origins

Two traps in the Ruy Lopez carry the name of Siegbert Tarrasch, and unlike many lines that exist only in analysis, he sprang both of them on masters in tournament games. He published his analysis of this one in 1891, and 18 months later Georg Marco fell into it in Tarrasch–Marco, Dresden 1892. Tarrasch spent just five minutes thinking during the whole game.

### The idea

4...Bd7 breaks the pin to meet the threat of 5.d5. 7.Re1 lays a subtle trap, and 7...exd4 is the better reply.

### The trap

7...O-O? 8.Bxc6 Bxc6 9.dxe5 dxe5 10.Qxd8 Raxd8 11.Nxe5 and White has won a pawn. Black's best is probably 11...Bd7, although White stays a pawn ahead. Marco went on 11...Bxe4 12.Nxe4 Nxe4, and now White can go wrong too: 13.Rxe4?? would be a horrible blunder because of 13...Rd1+ 14.Re1 Rxe1#. Tarrasch played 13.Nd3, which removes that possibility and makes the threat to the knight on e4 real. After 13...f5 the knight cannot move, since it is pinned against the bishop on e7. 14.f3 Bc5+?! (14...Bh4 15.g3 Nxg3 16.hxg3 Bxg3 gives Black two pawns for the knight) 15.Nxc5 Nxc5 16.Bg5 Rd5 17.Be7 Re8 18.c4, and Marco resigned, since White wins at least the exchange.

### The trap in the Open Defence

The other Tarrasch Trap is in the [Open Defence](/openings/ruy-lopez-open): 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 Nf6 5.O-O Nxe4 6.d4 b5 7.Bb3 d5 8.dxe5 Be6 9.c3 Be7 10.Re1 O-O 11.Nd4 Qd7? 12.Nxe6. However Black recaptures, the pawn on d5 is pinned, along the d-file or the a2–g8 diagonal, and after 12...Qxe6 or 12...fxe6 White wins a piece with 13.Rxe4. Two world championship challengers fell for it against Tarrasch: Johannes Zukertort at Frankfurt in 1887 and Isidor Gunsberg at Manchester in 1890.

### Who should know it

Steinitz Defence players with Black should remember that 7...exd4 comes before castling when White's rook is on e1. With White, the Steinitz position after 7.Re1 is worth reaching, since natural castling loses a pawn.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 60.9% of the 22,120 games and Black 32.2%, with 7.0% drawn.
