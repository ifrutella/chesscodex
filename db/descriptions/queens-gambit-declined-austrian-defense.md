The Austrian Defense is 1.d4 d5 2.c4 c5, an uncommon reply to the [Queen's Gambit](/openings/queens-gambit) that is also called the Symmetrical Defense. Black asks whether copying White's moves is enough to equalise. Most opening theoreticians believe that White should gain the advantage and that Black is at best playing for a draw.

### Origins

The opening was first described in print by Alessandro Salvio in 1604. The name arose because Austrian players studied it, among them Hans Haberditz (c. 1901–57), Hans Müller (1896–1971) and grandmaster Ernst Grünfeld.

Grandmaster Hikaru Nakamura used the opening a few times in 2023 and 2024. With it he drew against former world champion Magnus Carlsen in the finals of the Champions Chess Tour 2023 and beat the reigning world champion Ding Liren at Norway Chess 2024. At the 2024 World Rapid Chess Championship he defeated Luca Moroni and drew against Bogdan-Daniel Deac with it.

### Main lines

White often replies 3.cxd5. Other moves are playable and may transpose to better-known openings such as the [Queen's Gambit Accepted](/openings/queens-gambit-accepted) and the [Tarrasch Defense](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-tarrasch-defense).

After 3.cxd5 Black has two ways to win the pawn back:

- 3...Qxd5 is not advisable. Either 4.Nf3 cxd4 5.Nc3 Qa5 6.Nxd4 or 5...Qd8 6.Qxd4 Qxd4 7.Nxd4 gives White a big lead in development.
- 3...Nf6, the [Gusev Countergambit](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-austrian-defense-gusev-countergambit), intends to recapture on d5 with the knight. White should be able to keep the advantage with 4.Nf3 or 4.e4. Possible continuations are 4.Nf3 cxd4 5.Nxd4 Nxd5 6.e4 Nc7 and 4.e4 Nxe4 5.dxc5 Nxc5 6.Nc3 e6.

### Who should play it

The Austrian Defense is a surprise choice. Black accepts a position that theory rates as better for White and in return takes the opponent out of the well-known lines of the Queen's Gambit on move two.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.7% of 2.3 million games and Black 43.2%, with 5.1% drawn. White wins 54.9% under 1400, 52.8% between 1400 and 1799 and 51.5% between 1800 and 2199. From 2200 up the sides are almost level, 46.0% for White and 45.0% for Black, with 9.0% drawn. In 385 master games over the board White won 37.9%, Black 17.1%, and 44.9% were drawn.
