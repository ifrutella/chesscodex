The Tennison Gambit is 1.e4 d5 2.Nf3, a pawn sacrifice against the [Scandinavian Defense](/openings/scandinavian-defense). It also arises from the [Zukertort Opening](/openings/zukertort-opening) by 1.Nf3 d5 2.e4, and its code is A06. White gives up the e-pawn and plays for tactics after 2...dxe4 3.Ng5.

### Origins

The first player to research the opening in depth was the amateur Otto Mandrup Tennison (1834–1909). He was born in Denmark, studied in Germany and moved to the United States in 1854, where he played in the chess clubs of New Orleans. From the first half of the 20th century many strong players picked up the idea.

One of Tennison's own games, played in New Orleans in 1891, went 1.Nf3 d5 2.e4 dxe4 3.Ng5 f5 4.Bc4 Nh6 5.Nxh7 Rxh7 6.Qh5+ Kd7 7.Qg6 Rh8 8.Be6+ Kc6 9.Bxc8+ Qd6 10.Qe8+ Kb6 11.Qa4.

### The idea

The gambit is considered theoretically unsound: White sacrifices a pawn at once and gets no solid positional compensation for it. Black still has to play precisely, and can easily fall into one of several tactical traps that leave White ahead.

### Main lines

After 2...dxe4 3.Ng5 Black has three main replies:

- 3...Nf6 4.Bc4 e6 5.Nc3 a6 6.Ngxe4 Nxe4 7.Nxe4 b5 8.Be2 Bb7 9.Bf3, and White had the advantage in Ermenkov–Bonchev, Bulgaria 1970.
- 3...e5 favours Black, both after 4.Nxe4 f5 and after 4.h4 Be7 5.Bc4 Bxg5 6.Qh5 g6 7.Qxg5 f6.
- 3...Bf5 gives Black the better position. One continuation is 4.g4 Bg6 5.Bg2 h5, and Black keeps the advantage in a sharp position.

### Who should play it

The gambit is a choice for blitz and casual games, where an opponent who has never seen 3.Ng5 may go wrong in the first few moves. With Black it is enough to know one reply: 3...Bf5 and 3...e5 both leave Black better.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.6% of 9.3 million games and Black 47.3%, with 4.1% drawn. Under 1400 Black is ahead, 50.5% against 45.7%. Between 1400 and 1799 the sides are level, 48.2% for Black and 48.1% for White, and between 1800 and 2199 White leads 48.7% to 47.0%. From 2200 up Black wins 47.8% and White 46.1%. Only 23 master games are in the database, too few to judge.
