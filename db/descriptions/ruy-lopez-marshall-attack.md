The Marshall Attack (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 Nf6 5.O-O Be7 6.Re1 b5 7.Bb3 O-O 8.c3 d5) is an aggressive line in the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez). Black sacrifices a pawn to gain the initiative and a kingside attack. Between two well-prepared players the usual result is a draw.

### Origins

Its most famous game is Capablanca–Marshall, played at the Manhattan Chess Club in New York in 1918. Frank Marshall introduced the attack there and lost, but the idea became popular afterwards. It had been played before: in a game in Havana in 1893, four Cuban amateurs in consultation used it against the visiting Carl Walbrodt, and Marshall himself may have tried it in 1917. Boris Spassky developed it in his 1965 match against Mikhail Tal. Modern top players who use it include Levon Aronian and Ding Liren.

### The idea

After 9.exd5 Nxd5 10.Nxe5 Nxe5 11.Rxe5 c6 12.d4 Bd6 13.Re1 Qh4 14.g3 Qh3, White's kingside has lost the f3-knight as a defender, and Black has the initiative. Black's ideas include ...Bg4 with tempo, or ...f5 followed by ...f4. White is a pawn up and hopes to neutralise the initiative and reach an endgame with the extra pawn.

### Main lines

- [Modern main line](/openings/ruy-lopez-marshall-attack-modern-main-line) up to 14...Qh3, the heart of the opening, including the [Spassky Variation](/openings/ruy-lopez-marshall-attack-main-line-spassky-variation).
- The [Original Marshall](/openings/ruy-lopez-marshall-attack-original-marshall-attack) 11...Nf6, Marshall's move from 1918. Modern practice prefers 11...c6.
- [Steiner Variation](/openings/ruy-lopez-marshall-attack-steiner-variation) 9...e4, which is not considered sound.
- Anti-Marshall lines: White avoids the gambit on move eight. For many years the most popular was [8.a4](/openings/ruy-lopez-closed-anti-marshall), but once 8...b4 proved viable, 8.h3 gained in popularity. Other options are 8.d4, 8.d3 and 8.a3.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Excellent compensation for the pawn: a lasting initiative against White's king.
- At top level it is a reliable way for Black to reach a safe position.
- At club level, unprepared White players can be overwhelmed quickly.

**Weaknesses**

- A lot of concrete theory, much of it going deep into the middlegame.
- White can avoid it with Anti-Marshall lines, so Black still needs to know the Closed Ruy Lopez.
- At the top, many lines end in a draw, so it is not ideal when Black must win.

### Who should play it

The Marshall suits well-prepared 1...e5 players who like the initiative and don't mind giving up material. It is excellent against opponents who are not ready for its theory. It is less useful against White players who always play the Anti-Marshall.

### The numbers

On Lichess between 1400 and 2200, Black wins 53.6–55.4% and White 41%. Above 2200, Black still leads, 49% against 43.6%. Among masters over the board 76% of games are drawn, White wins 14% and Black 10%.
