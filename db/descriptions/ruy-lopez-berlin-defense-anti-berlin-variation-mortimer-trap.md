The Mortimer Trap is a trap in the [Berlin Defence](/openings/ruy-lopez-berlin-defense) of the Ruy Lopez: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 Nf6 4.d3 Ne7 5.Nxe5 c6. It is a true trap in the sense that Black plays an inferior move on purpose to tempt White into a mistake, and here White has just made it by taking on e5. The trap is named after James Mortimer; the code is C65.

### Origins

Mortimer played his defence at the London tournament of 1883 against Berthold Englisch, Samuel Rosenthal and Josef Noa, and lost all three games. Johannes Zukertort, who won the tournament, played it against Englisch and drew. Zukertort wrote of 4...Ne7: "Mr. Mortimer claims to be the inventor of this move. I adopted it on account of its novelty." I. A. Horowitz and Fred Reinfeld later called 4...Ne7 a "time-wasting retreat", but noted that the pitfall "had a vogue for many years".

### The idea

4.d3 is a quiet alternative to 4.O-O, 4.d4 or 4.Nc3; Horowitz and Reinfeld called it "Steinitz's move", with which he scored many successes as World Champion. 4...Ne7, the Mortimer Defence, aims to reroute the knight to g6. The move loses time and is objectively inferior, but it invites 5.Nxe5?.

### The trap

After 5.Nxe5 c6! Black attacks the bishop and threatens 6...Qa5+. If the bishop moves, with 6.Ba4 or 6.Bc4, 6...Qa5+ forks the king and the knight and wins a piece. 6.Nc4 covers a5 and threatens the smothered mate 7.Nd6#, but 6...Ng6! still wins material. 6...d6 also works: after 7.Ba4 the fork 7...b5 wins a piece for two pawns.

### What White should play

White has several acceptable answers to 4...Ne7, for example 5.Bc4 c6 6.Nc3 Ng6 7.h4, with the upper hand and an attack according to Keres and Olafsson. The first edition of *Chess Openings, Ancient and Modern* analysed 5.Nc3 Ng6 6.O-O c6 7.Ba4 d6 8.Bb3 and gave Black an equal game with 8...Be6 or 8...Be7.

### Who should know it

Players who meet the Berlin with 4.d3 should know it before grabbing the e5-pawn. With Black, 4...Ne7 is a gamble: it only pays off if White takes on e5.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 63.4% of the 31,476 games and White 33.4%, with 3.2% drawn.
