The Barnes Opening is 1.f3, an irregular first move classified as A00 and sometimes called Gedult's Opening. It develops no piece, takes the f3-square away from the king's knight and weakens White's kingside.

### Origins

The opening is named after Thomas Wilson Barnes (1825–1874), an English player who won eight games against Paul Morphy. In one of them Barnes answered 1.e4 with 1...f6, a move now known as the [Barnes Defense](/openings/barnes-defense).

### The idea

The move does have some influence on the central square e4, but almost any other first move achieves as much or more. 1.f3 develops nothing, opens no lines for the pieces and takes away the most natural square of the king's knight. It also weakens the kingside pawns, opens the e1–h4 diagonal against White's uncastled king and opens the g1–a7 diagonal against a king that castles short.

Grandmaster Edmar Mednis argued that 1.f3 is the worst of the twenty possible first moves, and grandmaster Benjamin Finegold teaches "Never play f3". Black can secure a comfortable advantage by normal means: advancing the central pawns and developing the pieces quickly.

### Main lines

- 1...e5 2.Kf2 is the [Hammerschlag](/openings/barnes-opening-hammerschlag), one of several move orders known as the Bongcloud Attack; this site lists 1.e4 e5 2.Ke2 under [that name](/openings/bongcloud-attack). Simon Williams beat Martin Simons with 2.Kf2 in the last round of the British Championship 1999, and in 2020 Magnus Carlsen played it against Wesley So in a blitz game for the psychological effect. If White plays poorly afterwards the king can be mated quickly: 1.f3 d5 2.Kf2 e5 3.e4 Bc5+ 4.Kg3 Qg5#.
- 1...e5 2.e4 is called the King's Head Opening.
- 1...e5 2.Nh3 d5 3.Nf2 Nf6 4.e3 Nc6 5.Be2 Bc5 6.O-O O-O is a passive line known as the Blue Moon Defence. White has no stake in the centre.
- 1...e5 2.g4 Qh4# is the [fool's mate](/openings/barnes-opening-fools-mate). After 1.f3 e5 it is the only move that allows mate in one. Another, 2.h3, allows mate in two: 2...Qh4+ 3.g3 Qxg3#.

### Who should play it

1.f3 is played for fun or for effect, as in Carlsen's blitz game. A player who opens with it should at least know how quickly the king can be mated after 2.Kf2 or 2.g4.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 51.9% of 1.6 million games and White 43.8%, with 4.3% drawn. Black wins 55.8% both under 1400 and between 1400 and 1799, where White wins 38.1% and 39.9%. Between 1800 and 2199 it is 50.8% against 44.8%, and from 2200 up 49.6% against 44.5%. Only 12 master games are in the database, too few to judge.
