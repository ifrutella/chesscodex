The Würzburger Trap is a trap in the [Vienna Gambit](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit): 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Nf6 3.f4 d5 4.fxe5 Nxe4 5.d3 Qh4+ 6.g3 Nxg3 7.Nf3 Qh5 8.Nxd5. The trap begins with Black's 5...Qh4+, and it is Black who can fall into it: after 8...Bg4 9.Nf4 a series of captures ends with 11...Bxc2?, and the bishop is trapped on c2. The code is C29.

### Origins

The trap was named around 1930 after the German banker Max Würzburger.

### The idea

3...d5 is thought to be the best reply to the gambit. After 4.fxe5 Nxe4 White's alternatives to 5.d3, 5.Qf3 (Steinitz) and 5.Nf3, do not achieve an advantage. 5...Qh4+ starts the trap; Black also has 5...Bb4 and 5...Nxc3. After 6.g3 Nxg3 7.Nf3 Qh5 8.Nxd5, taking the rook with 8...Nxh1? is a mistake: 9.Nxc7+ Kd8 10.Nxa8 gives White the advantage.

### The trap

8...Bg4 9.Nf4 Bxf3 10.Nxh5 Bxd1 11.hxg3 Bxc2? Black tries to win a pawn but loses a piece: after 12.b3 the bishop on c2 is trapped, and White wins it next move with Kd2 or Rh2.

### Better for White

White can get the better game with 9.Bg2 instead of 9.Nf4. One line is 9...Nxh1 10.Nxc7+ Kd7 11.Nxa8 Nc6; after 10...Kd8 11.Nxa8 Nc6 12.d4 Bxf3 13.Qxf3 Qxf3 14.Bxf3 Nxd4 15.Bg5+ Be7 16.Rd1 Larsen assesses the position as winning for White. 9...Bxf3 10.Qxf3 Qxe5+ 11.Kd1 Nxh1 12.Bf4 Qxb2 13.Qe4+ was played in Hamann–Schvenkrantz, Germany 1965.

### Who should know it

Black players who meet the Vienna Gambit with 3...d5 and 5...Qh4+ should know it: after 9.Nf4 the capture 11...Bxc2? loses a piece. With White, 9.Bg2 gives the better game.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 67.6% of the 17,850 games and Black 29.0%, with 3.4% drawn.
