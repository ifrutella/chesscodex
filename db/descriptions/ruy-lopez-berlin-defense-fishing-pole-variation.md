The Fishing Pole is a trap that Black sets in the [Berlin Defence](/openings/ruy-lopez-berlin-defense) with 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 Nf6 4.O-O Ng4. In its broad sense it is any sacrifice of a knight or bishop on the g-file that opens the h-file after the opponent has castled short and before the player setting the trap, usually Black, has done so. It is most common in the Ruy Lopez, in the Berlin and the [Exchange Variation](/openings/ruy-lopez-exchange-variation). The code is C65.

### Origins

The earliest known example is from the middlegame of Zukertort–Winawer at the London tournament of 1883, which Zukertort won. The trap also appeared at move 12 of game 8 of the 2023 World Championship between Ian Nepomniachtchi and Ding Liren.

### The idea

4...Ng4 is objectively a poor move: the knight leaves the centre, and after 5.h3 it would have to go back to f6 with the loss of a tempo, which lets White win the e5-pawn with 5...Nf6 6.Bxc6 dxc6 7.Nxe5. The trap is 5...h5. The name describes it: the knight on g4 is the bait and the h5-pawn is the hook. If White takes the bait, the h-file opens towards White's king.

### Main lines

- 5.h3 h5 6.hxg4?? hxg4 7.Nh2?? Qh4 leads to mate. For example 8.f3 g3 9.Rf2 Nd4 10.Kf1 gxh2 11.Ke1 h1=Q+ 12.Bf1 Qxf1+ 13.Kxf1 Qh1#, or 8.Re1 Qxh2+ 9.Kf1 Nd4 10.f4 g3 11.Bxd7+ Bxd7 12.Qh5 Qh1+ 13.Qxh1 Rxh1#. 8.f4 g3 9.Rf3 Qxh2+ also loses, if more slowly.
- The same idea works in the Exchange Variation: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Bxc6 dxc6 5.O-O Bg4 6.h3 h5. There 7.hxg4? hxg4 8.Nxe5?? Qh4 9.f4 g3 10.Qh5 Rxh5 11.Rf3 Qh1# shows the danger; 7.d3 is the right move there.

### Strengths and weaknesses

- Against an opponent who grabs the knight, the attack down the h-file can be decisive within a few moves.
- Against careful play 4...Ng4 just loses time.

### Who should know it

It suits fast games against opponents who grab material. With White, do not take a piece on g4 when Black's h-pawn has reached h5 and Black's king has not castled.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 52.2% of the 55,863 games and Black 44.5%, with 3.3% drawn.
