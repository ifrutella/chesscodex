The Stafford Gambit (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.Nxe5 Nc6) is a trappy sideline of [Petrov's Defense](/openings/petrovs-defense). Black gives up the e5-pawn for quick development and a series of tactical traps. It is objectively dubious, but its traps have proved effective under fast time controls online.

### Origins

The earliest recorded game with the line comes from a simultaneous exhibition in 1857 by Howard Staunton, who had Black and lost. It is named after Joseph Stafford, a correspondence player who won a notable six-move game with it in 1950. For decades it was rarely seen in tournament play. In the early 2020s it became popular in online blitz through the games of the American International Master Eric Rosen. In December 2024 Rosen used it to beat grandmaster Sergey Erenburg in 14 moves at the FIDE World Blitz Championship.

### The idea

After 4.Nxc6 dxc6, Black is a pawn down, but has open lines for the bishops and queen. Black usually continues ...Bc5, and the traps often involve ...Ng4 or a sacrifice on f2. White's natural moves often run into tactics. The best-known example is the "Rosen Trap": after 5.d3 Bc5 6.Bg5? Nxe4!, taking the queen with 7.Bxd8 loses to 7...Bxf2+ 8.Ke2 Bg4#.

### Main lines

After 4.Nxc6 dxc6, White's main continuations are:

- **5.d3**: the most solid move, protecting e4. White must still avoid 6.Bg5? and be careful about ...Ng4 ideas against f2.
- **5.Nc3**: a flexible developing move.
- **5.e5**: aggressive but risky; Black's knight usually jumps to e4.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Many traps. Unprepared opponents lose quickly, especially in blitz and bullet.
- Simple plans for Black: fast development and pressure on f2.
- It takes White out of normal Petrov theory from move three.

**Weaknesses**

- Objectively unsound: engines rate the position about +1.5 to +2 for White.
- Well-prepared opponents simply keep the pawn and develop normally.
- In classical games it is generally considered unsuitable for serious use.

### Who should play it

The Stafford is a fast-chess weapon for players who enjoy traps and quick attacks and don't mind being objectively worse. It works best against opponents who make "natural" moves without checking tactics. Against prepared players, or in slow games, a solid defence such as the main-line [Petrov](/openings/petrovs-defense) is the better choice.

### The numbers

On Lichess, below 1800, Black wins 50.9–51.5% against White's 45.3–45.9%. From 1800 up, the advantage switches to White, with 48.3–48.5% against 45.7–47.6%. There are almost no master games with it (only a dozen in the database).
