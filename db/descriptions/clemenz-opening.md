The Clemenz Opening is 1.h3, an irregular first move classified as A00. It makes no claim on the central squares and does not help development, and it is among the rarest of the 20 possible first moves.

### Origins

The opening is named after Hermann Clemenz (1846–1908), an Estonian player. International Master Michael Basman has experimented with 1.h3. He usually followed it with 2.g4, which transposes to the [Grob](/openings/grob-opening), or with 2.a3 and a quick c4, a line that has been dubbed the "Creepy Crawly" and is also known as the Global Opening.

### The idea

Like [Anderssen's Opening](/openings/anderssens-opening), 1.a3, the move 1.h3 wastes time. It also weakens White's kingside slightly, though not as badly as 1.g4 or the [Barnes Opening](/openings/barnes-opening), 1.f3.

### Main lines

Black has a number of playable replies:

- 1...d5 and 1...e5 are the most common and stake a claim to central space. This site lists 1...e5 2.a3 as the [Global Opening](/openings/global-opening) and 1...d5 2.a3 e5 as the [Creepy Crawly Formation](/openings/creepy-crawly-formation-classical-defense).
- 1...b6, or even 1...b5, prepares a fianchetto that puts pressure on White's weakened pawns and forestalls a kingside expansion with g4.
- 1...f5 is probably not Black's best reply, since 2.d4 transposes to a sharp line against the Dutch Defense once tried by Viktor Korchnoi, the [Korchnoi Attack](/openings/dutch-defense-korchnoi-attack).
- 1...h5 2.g4 is the [Spike Lee Gambit](/openings/clemenz-opening-spike-lee-gambit).

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 50.9% of 1.05 million games and White 44.4%, with 4.7% drawn. Under 1400 Black wins 52.5% and White 40.3%, and between 1400 and 1799 it is 52.8% against 42.0%. Between 1800 and 2199 Black leads 49.8% to 45.6%. From 2200 up White is ahead, 48.0% against 45.6%. In 132 master games over the board Black won 37.9%, White 27.3%, and 34.8% were drawn.
