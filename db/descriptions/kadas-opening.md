The Kádas Opening is 1.h4, an irregular first move classified as A00 and also known as the Desprez Opening. It does nothing in the fight for the centre, does very little for development and weakens White's kingside.

### Origins

The name Desprez Opening comes from the French player Marcel Després. Like a number of other rare openings, 1.h4 has further names: Anti-Borg Opening, Samurai Opening, Harry's Opening and Reagan's Attack.

Grandmaster David Bronstein once remarked that he knew of a Russian player who always opened with 1.h4 and always won. His point was the line 1...e5 2.g3 d5 3.d4 exd4 4.Qxd4 Nc6 5.Qd1 Nf6 6.Nh3 Be7 7.Nf4 O-O 8.Bg2, in which the knight is well placed on f4 and White has a good position. Black does not have to be so cooperative, however. Hikaru Nakamura has used the opening on many occasions, particularly in his games of 2024.

### The idea

Like 1.a4, the [Ware Opening](/openings/ware-opening), 1.h4 takes no central space. The only piece it releases is the rook, which is usually not developed to h3. For these reasons, and because of the weakened kingside, it is among the rarest of the twenty possible first moves.

### Main lines

Black usually takes the centre with 1...d5 or 1...e5. Simple development with 1...Nf6 is also sound. 1...g6, planning a fianchetto on g7, is rare, because White can undermine Black's pawns with 2.h5.

The named lines are:

- 1...c5 2.b4, the [Kádas Gambit](/openings/kadas-opening-kadas-gambit). The name is also used for [1...e5 2.d4 exd4 3.c3](/openings/kadas-opening-kadas-gambit-2).
- 1...a5, the [Koola-Koola Variation](/openings/kadas-opening-koola-koola-variation).
- 1...d5 2.d4 c5 3.e4, the [Myers Variation](/openings/kadas-opening-myers-variation).
- 1...g5, the [Schneider Gambit](/openings/kadas-opening-schneider-gambit).
- 1...f5 2.e4 fxe4 3.d3, the [Steinbok Gambit](/openings/kadas-opening-steinbok-gambit).
- 1...d5 2.Rh3, the Beginner's Mistake, listed here as the [Beginner's Trap](/openings/kadas-opening-beginners-trap): Black's bishop can take the rook with 2...Bxh3.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 51.0% of 1.4 million games and White 44.4%, with 4.5% drawn. Under 1400 Black wins 54.8% and White 37.3%, and between 1400 and 1799 it is 55.2% against 39.2%. Between 1800 and 2199 Black still leads, 49.5% against 46.2%. From 2200 up White is ahead, 48.1% against 46.1%. Only 53 master games are in the database, too few to judge.
