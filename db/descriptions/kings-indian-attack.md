The King's Indian Attack is a system for White built with Nf3, g3, Bg2, O-O, d3, Nbd2 and e4. It is the same setup Black uses in the [King's Indian Defense](/openings/kings-indian-defense), but with an extra move. On this site the name covers 1.Nf3 d5 2.g3, but the system can be reached from many move orders, including against the French and the Sicilian.

### Origins

The King's Indian Attack is named after the King's Indian Defence, whose setup White adopts. It emerged in the 1950s, when grandmasters who appreciated Black's King's Indian system began to use it with the extra move. Players who used it include Bobby Fischer (mostly early in his career), Mikhail Botvinnik, Vasily Smyslov, Tigran Petrosian and Leonid Stein. Today it is relatively uncommon at grandmaster level, and its most frequent high-level practitioner is the Egyptian grandmaster Bassem Amin. It is popular at club level.

### The idea

White aims for the same set-up against various defences: Nf3, g3, Bg2, O-O, d3, Nbd2 and e4. The most common plan, especially against setups with ...e6, is the push e4–e5, gaining a central bind and kingside space and giving practical attacking chances against Black's castled king. Unlike in the French Defence, White's e5-pawn is supported by pieces rather than by a d4-pawn.

### Typical Black setups

- **With ...e6 and ...c5** (as in the French or Sicilian): the set-ups the KIA is most often used against. After e4–e5 White continues with moves such as Re1, Nf1, Bf4, h4 and N1h2 or Ne3, with a kingside attack.
- **With the c8-bishop outside the pawn chain**: on g4 it is the Keres System, for example 1.Nf3 d5 2.g3 Nf6 3.Bg2 c6 4.O-O Bg4; on f5 it gives a reversed London System.
- **With ...c5, ...Nc6 and ...e5**: the Reversed King's Indian, for example 1.Nf3 d5 2.g3 c5 3.Bg2 Nc6 4.O-O e5 5.d3 Nf6.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- One setup against almost everything, with very little theory to learn.
- Clear attacking plans with e5 and a kingside pawn storm.
- A safe king position and a flexible middlegame.

**Weaknesses**

- Objectively modest: Black equalises easily with sensible play.
- Black's queenside counterplay can arrive before White's attack.
- Its predictability allows well-prepared opponents to choose the most unpleasant setup.

### Who should play it

The KIA suits players who want a universal system with White, prefer understanding to memorising, and like kingside attacks in closed positions. It is effective against French- and Sicilian-type setups with ...e6. It is less effective against opponents who build a solid ...d5 and ...c6 centre and aim for quiet equality.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 49.7–50.8% and Black 44.9–45.8% below 1800. Between 1800 and 2200 White does better, 53.1% against 41.3%, and above 2200 it is 50.6% against 40.9%. In master games over the board White wins 34.2%, Black 22.0%, and 43.8% are drawn.
