The Vienna Gambit is 1.e4 e5 2.Nc3 Nf6 3.f4, the classical reply to the [Falkbeer Variation](/openings/vienna-game-falkbeer-variation) of the [Vienna Game](/openings/vienna-game). White offers the f-pawn, and Black usually declines it and strikes back in the centre with 3...d5.

### The name

Traditionally the term "Vienna Gambit" applies to 3.f4 after 2...Nc6, which this site lists as the [Vienna Gambit, with Max Lange Defense](/openings/vienna-gambit-with-max-lange-defense). Online the name is frequently used for the same pawn move after 2...Nf6, the line on this page.

### The idea

Black should not accept the gambit. After 3...exf4 4.e5 the knight has to go back to g8; 4...Qe7 is even weaker, because 5.Qe2 forces 5...Ng8 anyway. Black usually plays 3...d5 instead, an idea similar to the [Falkbeer Countergambit](/openings/kings-gambit-declined-falkbeer-countergambit) of the King's Gambit. The classical main line continues 4.fxe5 Nxe4 and reaches a sharp position.

Anthony Santasiere and Weaver W. Adams, among others, advocated the gambit. Raymond Keene wrote that it is considered too risky at grandmaster level.

### Main lines

After 3...d5 4.fxe5 Nxe4 three fifth moves are played often:

- 5.Nf3, the traditional main move. White obtains open lines and attacking chances, and Black can usually hold the balance with correct play. Black's most common reply is 5...Be7, the [Breyer Variation](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit-breyer-variation), which most often continues 6.Qe2 Nxc3 7.dxc3 c5 8.Bf4 Nc6 9.O-O-O Be6. White then often attacks on the kingside with h4, and Black often counterattacks on the queenside with ...Qa5 at some point. Other replies are 5...Nc6, 5...Bc5, which threatens 6...Bf2+, and 5...Bg4, usually followed by 6.Qe2, the [Kaufmann Variation](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit-kaufmann-variation).
- 5.Qf3, the [Paulsen Attack](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit-paulsen-attack), also known as Spielmann's line. It develops the queen aggressively and has been advocated by Ben Finegold. Black usually answers 5...Nxc3, 5...Nc6 or 5...f5, the [Bardeleben Variation](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit-bardeleben-variation). After 5...Nc6 play most often continues 6.Bb5 Nxc3 7.dxc3 Qh4+ 8.g3 Qe4+ 9.Qxe4 dxe4.
- 5.d3, the [Modern Variation](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit-modern-variation), also called the Oxford Variation. It most often continues 5...Nxc3 6.bxc3 d4 7.Nf3 Nc6. After 5...Qh4+ it can lead to the [Würzburger Trap](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit-wurzburger-trap).

White's most notable alternative on move four is 4.exd5. Black usually continues 4...Nxd5, 4...exf4 or 4...e4. After 4...Nxd5, 5.Nxd5 Qxd5 is fine for Black, while 5.fxe5 Nxc3 6.bxc3 Qh4+ forces White either to give up a rook with 7.g3 Qe4+ or to play 7.Ke2. 4...exf4 5.Nf3 transposes to the [Modern Defense](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-modern-defense) of the King's Gambit Accepted. 4.d3 is the [Steinitz Variation](/openings/vienna-game-vienna-gambit-steinitz-variation), usually followed by 4...exf4 or 4...d4.

Apart from 3...d5, Black's only sound move is 3...d6, a line of the King's Gambit Declined that most often continues 4.Nf3. 3...Nc6 is weak because of 4.fxe5 Nxe5 5.d4, when both 5...Nc6 and 5...Ng6 are met by 6.e5 with a strong attack.

### Who should play it

- The gambit suits players who like open, tactical positions and are willing to learn a few sharp lines.
- With Black the first thing to know is 3...d5 and the main line 4.fxe5 Nxe4.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 55.5% of 4.3 million games and Black 40.4%, with 4.1% drawn. White wins 58.0% under 1400, 57.4% between 1400 and 1799, 54.9% between 1800 and 2199 and 50.4% from 2200 up, where Black wins 42.7%. In 1,210 master games over the board White won 29.0%, Black 30.4%, and 40.6% were drawn.
