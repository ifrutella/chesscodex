The Rice Gambit is 1.e4 e5 2.f4 exf4 3.Nf3 g5 4.h4 g4 5.Ne5 Nf6 6.Bc4 d5 7.exd5 Bd6 8.O-O, an offshoot of the [Kieseritzky Gambit](/openings/kings-gambit-accepted-kieseritzky-gambit). Instead of the normal 8.d4, White castles and offers the knight on e5, to bring the king to safety and a rook into the attack against Black's underdeveloped position. The code is C39.

### Origins

The gambit was heavily promoted at the end of the 19th century by Isaac Rice, a wealthy German-born American businessman. He sponsored many theme tournaments in which every game started from the position after 8.O-O, with participants such as Emanuel Lasker, Mikhail Chigorin, Carl Schlechter, Frank Marshall and David Janowski. The events ranged from Monte Carlo, Saint Petersburg and Ostend to Brooklyn and Trenton Falls. In a 1905 event of the Pillsbury National Correspondence Chess Association, 230 amateurs played the gambit by mail. In 1904 Rice formed The Rice Gambit Association at his home in New York; with Lasker as its secretary, it even published a book of all the games from the theme tournaments.

### The idea

Concrete analysis long ago showed the gambit to be "neither good nor necessary", so it has been abandoned in serious play and stands only as "a grotesque monument to a rich man's vanity". Gallagher wrote in 1992 that, according to theory, White "miraculously holds the balance", but still advised readers to steer clear of it.

### Main lines

- The Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings (1997) analyses 8...Bxe5 9.Re1 Qe7 10.c3 Nh5 11.d4 Nd7 12.dxe5 Nxe5 13.b3 O-O 14.Ba3 Nf3+ 15.gxf3 Qxh4 16.Re5 Bf5 17.Nd2 Qg3+ 18.Kf1 Qh2 19.Bxf8 g3 20.Bc5 g2+ 21.Ke1 Qh4+ 22.Ke2 Ng3+ 23.Kf2 Ne4+, a draw by perpetual check, and credits this analysis to José Raúl Capablanca, Amos Burn and Edward Lasker.
- Shaw (2013) gives 8...Bxe5 9.Re1 Qe7! 10.c3 f3! 11.d4 Ne4! 12.Rxe4 Bh2+ 13.Kxh2 Qxe4, an "accurate (and by no means obvious) sequence" after which Black is better.

### Strengths and weaknesses

- White's attack is fast and dangerous, and the defence is not obvious over the board.
- With accurate play Black can at least hold, and modern analysis gives Black the better game.

### Who should play it

It is for players interested in the history of the King's Gambit and in sharp theme positions. With Black, the Shaw line 9...Qe7 and 10...f3 is worth knowing.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 59.5% of the 148 games and White 36.5%, with 4.1% drawn.
