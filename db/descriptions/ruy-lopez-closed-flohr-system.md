The Zaitsev Variation of the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez), also called the Flohr–Zaitsev Variation, starts with 9...Bb7 in the [Closed Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez-closed): 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 Nf6 5.O-O Be7 6.Re1 b5 7.Bb3 d6 8.c3 O-O 9.h3 Bb7. Lichess calls this position the Flohr System. The code is C92.

### Origins

Igor Zaitsev, for many years one of Anatoly Karpov's trainers, advocated the variation, and Karpov played it often. It remains one of the most important variations of the Ruy Lopez. Karpov chose 12...Qd7, after 12.a4, against Garry Kasparov in the World Championship matches of 1984 and 1985.

### The idea

5...Be7 enters the Closed Defence instead of the sharp [Open Defence](/openings/ruy-lopez-open), 5...Nxe4, and the traditional continuation 6.Re1 b5 7.Bb3 d6 8.c3 O-O 9.h3 avoids further sharp lines such as the [Marshall Attack](/openings/ruy-lopez-marshall-attack). With 9...Bb7 Black prepares to add pressure on e4 after 10.d4 Re8. One drawback is that White can force a draw by repetition with 11.Ng5 Rf8 12.Nf3.

### Main lines

After 10.d4 Re8 the usual move is 11.Nbd2, and Black almost always answers 11...Bf8. 11.a3, 11.a4 and the drawing 11.Ng5 are also seen. After 11...Bf8, 12.Nf1? loses a pawn to 12...exd4 13.cxd4 Na5 14.Bc2 Nxe4. White's choices are:

- 12.a4, the main move, which presses on Black's queenside. The main line 12...h6 13.Bc2 exd4 14.cxd4 Nb4 15.Bb1 c5 16.d5 Nd7 17.Ra3 c4 18.axb5 axb5 19.Nd4 Rxa3 20.bxa3 Nd3 21.Bxd3 cxd3 22.Re3 Nc5 23.Bb2 Qa5 24.Nf5 g6 25.Ng3 is where theory ends. It is not clear that White has an advantage there: Black has the bishop pair and a passed pawn on d3, which is why White has turned to other twelfth moves more recently. 12...Na5 and 12...Qd7 are the other replies.
- 12.a3 controls b4, stops ...Nb4 ideas and frees a2 for the bishop. After 12...h6 13.Bc2 Nb8 White chooses between 14.b3 and 14.b4.
- 12.Bc2 is the old move. White protects e4 and aims for d5, c4 and b3, while Black presses on the queenside with ideas such as ...Nb8–d7, ...Qc7, ...c6 and ...a5.
- 12.d5 Nb8 13.Nf1 Nbd7 opens the game more; White then picks between 14.N3h2 and 14.Ng3.

Other tenth moves: 10.d3 is a quieter option, and 10.a4 is rare. Black can also deviate on move 10: 10...Nd7 may transpose to the Karpov Variation and 10...h6 to the Smyslov Variation.

### Strengths and weaknesses

- The bishop on b7 and the rook on e8 put steady pressure on e4.
- The line is rich in theory, and in the main 12.a4 line Black gets the bishop pair and a passed d-pawn.
- White has a forced draw with 11.Ng5 Rf8 12.Nf3, so Black cannot avoid it if White is happy with a draw.

### Who should play it

It suits Black players who already play the Closed Ruy Lopez and are ready to learn long main lines. With White you need an answer to the main line of 12.a4; 12.a3, 12.Bc2 and 12.d5 are alternatives with their own plans.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.5% of the 131,250 games and Black 43.3%, with 6.2% drawn.
