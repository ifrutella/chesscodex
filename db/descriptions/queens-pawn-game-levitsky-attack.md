The Levitsky Attack, better known as the Hodgson Attack, is 1.d4 d5 2.Bg5. It is also called the Pseudo-Trompowsky, the Queen's Bishop Attack and the Bishop Attack, and the Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings covers it under D00. White brings the queen's bishop out before the knights and before c2–c4.

### Origins

Preston Ware tested the opening in the 1880s. The name Levitsky comes from Stepan Levitsky; the name Hodgson comes from the English grandmaster Julian Hodgson, who studied and played the line a great deal, found several new ideas in it and scored well with it. The opening was very rare until the 1980s, when several players, Michael Adams and Tony Miles among them, tried it. It is still a sideline next to the [Queen's Gambit](/openings/queens-gambit), 2.c4.

### The idea

The bishop on g5 pins the e-pawn and is ready to answer 2...Nf6 with 3.Bxf6: White gives up the bishop pair and leaves Black with doubled pawns. The wider aim is to provoke weaknesses on Black's kingside. Modern Chess Openings treats the line as a variation of the [Trompowsky Attack](/openings/trompowsky-attack), although that name usually means 1.d4 Nf6 2.Bg5.

### Main lines

- 2...Nf6 3.Bxf6 goes along with White's idea and leads to a playable variation of the Trompowsky.
- 2...h6 3.Bh4 c6 is a solid way to chase the bishop. Black follows with ...Qb6 and attacks the pawn on b2, which the bishop on h4 no longer defends.
- 2...c5, 2...g6, 2...c6 and 2...f6, which chases the bishop at once, are also possible.
- 2...Bg4, the [Welling Variation](/openings/queens-pawn-game-levitsky-attack-welling-variation), is an unusual reply that copies White's move.

### Strengths and weaknesses

- White avoids the main lines of the Queen's Gambit and sets Black problems from move two.
- The exchange on f6 leaves Black with doubled pawns, but White hands over the bishop pair to get them.
- With the dark-squared bishop gone from c1, b2 is a target, and ...Qb6 hits it in many lines.

### Who should play it

It suits White players who want a less explored system after 1.d4 d5 and do not mind giving up the bishop for a knight. Black does not need a special answer: ...h6, ...c6 and ...Qb6 is a solid plan that aims straight at the weak b2-pawn.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 53.3% of 5.5 million games and Black 42.0%, with 4.8% drawn. White's lead is smallest under 1400, 49.6% against 46.1%; between 1400 and 1799 White wins 53.6% and between 1800 and 2199 53.2%. From 2200 up White wins 51.3% and Black 41.5%. In 2,791 master games White wins 35.0%, Black 26.7%, and 38.4% are drawn.
