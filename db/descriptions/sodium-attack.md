The Sodium Attack is 1.Na3, a rarely played first move classified as A00 and also known as the Durkin Opening or the Durkin Attack. The name comes from the notation: the move is written Na3, and Na is the chemical symbol for sodium.

### Origins

The opening is named for Robert T. Durkin (1923–2014) of New Jersey. In the same spirit the [Amar Opening](/openings/amar-opening), 1.Nh3, is sometimes referred to as the Ammonia Opening.

### The idea

This development of the queen's knight does little to use White's advantage of the first move. On a3 the knight controls no central square, and White would have to move it again, to c2 or c4 for example, for it to follow common opening principles. The knight also runs the risk of being captured by Black's dark-squared bishop from f8, which in some positions leaves White with doubled pawns, a weakness especially in the endgame.

Angus Dunnington suggests that combining 1.Na3 with a gradual central expansion should give White a reasonable position. White may also follow up with c4, for example 1...d5 2.c4 dxc4 3.Nxc4.

### Main lines

- 1...e5 2.Nc4 Nc6 3.e4 f5 is the [Durkin Gambit](/openings/sodium-attack-durkin-gambit).
- 1...g6 2.g4 is listed on this site as the [Chenoboskion Variation](/openings/sodium-attack-chenoboskion-variation).
- 1...e5 2.d3 Bxa3 3.bxa3 d5 4.e3 c5 5.Rb1, the [Celadon Variation](/openings/sodium-attack-celadon-variation), shows the capture on a3 and the doubled pawns it leaves.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.0% of about 157,000 games and Black 45.8%, with 4.2% drawn. This site has no figures by rating band or from master games for this line.
