The Noah's Ark Trap is a family of traps in the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez) in which Black's pawns shut White's bishop in on b3. This page is the line 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 b5 5.Bb3 d6 6.d4 Nxd4 7.Nxd4 exd4 8.Qxd4 c5, after which ...c4 traps the bishop. The code is C71.

### Origins

The origin of the name is uncertain. The black pawns on a6, b5 and c4 may look like an ark, or the name may mean that the trap is "as old as Noah's Ark". Masters have occasionally fallen into it. In Steiner–Capablanca, Budapest 1929, the game went 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 d6 5.d4 b5 6.Bb3 Nxd4 7.Nxd4 exd4 8.Qxd4 c5 9.Qd5 Be6 10.Qc6+ Bd7 11.Qd5 c4, and the bishop was trapped. White resigned after 32 moves.

### The idea

After 6.d4 Nxd4 7.Nxd4 exd4 8.Qxd4 the white queen stands in the centre; ...c5 drives it away, and ...c4 then shuts the bishop in on b3. 8.Qxd4 was recommended by Alexander Alekhine in the tournament book of New York 1924 as a way for White to draw, but it is a mistake that loses material. White should play 8.Bd5 or try the gambit 8.c3 instead. In the Steiner game White also had better options on move 5: 5.c3, 5.Bxc6+ and 5.O-O.

### In the Sicilian

A version of the trap arises in the [Rossolimo](/openings/sicilian-defense-nyezhmetdinov-rossolimo-attack): 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4?? b5 5.Bb3 c4, and the bishop is trapped in the same way. 4.Bxc6 is necessary there; it is the point of 3.Bb5.

### Who should know it

Ruy Lopez players with White should know it: after an early d4 and Qxd4, the bishop on b3 can be cut off by ...c5 and ...c4. With Black it is worth remembering in the [Modern Steinitz](/openings/ruy-lopez-morphy-defense-modern-steinitz-defense) and similar lines with ...a6, ...b5 and ...d6.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 71.0% of the 54,434 games and White 23.7%, with 5.3% drawn.
