The Alekhine-Chatard Attack is 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 Be7 5.e5 Nfd7 6.h4, a gambit in the [Classical Variation](/openings/french-defense-classical-variation) of the French Defense. White leaves the bishop on g5 to be taken and offers a pawn in order to keep Black's king in the centre.

### Origins

The gambit was devised by Adolf Albin and played by Chatard, but it was not taken seriously until the game Alekhine–Fahrni, Mannheim 1914. Today it is known as the Albin–Chatard Attack or the Alekhine–Chatard Attack. It is also called the Chatard–Alekhine Attack, the Albin Attack and the Alekhine Attack.

### The idea

After 6...Bxg5 7.hxg5 Qxg5 8.Nh3 Qe7 9.Qg4 g6 10.Ng5 White has sacrificed a pawn to keep the black king in the centre, as castling appears safe on neither side. The knight goes to h3 and not to f3 so that the queen can come to g4.

The gambit has a second point. Black's natural French move 6...c5 runs into 7.Bxe7. Black must then either move the king with 7...Kxe7 or allow 7...Qxe7 8.Nb5, which threatens both Nc7+, winning the rook on a8, and Nd6+, after which the king has to move and the knight is very strong on d6.

### Main lines

- 6...Bxg5 7.hxg5 Qxg5 accepts the pawn and is listed here as the [Albin-Chatard Gambit](/openings/french-defense-alekhine-chatard-attack-albin-chatard-gambit). Play continues 8.Nh3 Qe7 9.Qg4 g6 10.Ng5.
- 6...a6, the [Maróczy Variation](/openings/french-defense-alekhine-chatard-attack-maroczy-variation), declines. White can keep attacking with 7.Qg4, which threatens Bxe7 followed by Qxg7. Black is forced to remove the bishop with 7...Bxg5 8.hxg5, and the h-file is opened. A wild game with unsafe kings follows.
- 6...h6 is a safer way to decline. It forces the exchange 7.Bxe7 Qxe7, after which White may still try to attack on the kingside with 8.f4 a6 9.g4. Black is expected to castle there, since castling queenside is undesirable when Black needs to play ...c5.
- 6...c5, the [Breyer Variation](/openings/french-defense-alekhine-chatard-attack-breyer-variation), runs into 7.Bxe7.

This site also lists 6...O-O as the [Spielmann Variation](/openings/french-defense-alekhine-chatard-attack-spielmann-variation) and 6...f6 as the [Teichmann Variation](/openings/french-defense-alekhine-chatard-attack-teichmann-variation).

### Who should play it

- For White it is the attacking alternative to the old main line 6.Bxe7 Qxe7.
- Black players of the Classical French need an answer ready. Taking the pawn means keeping the king in the centre for a long time, and 6...h6 is the calmer choice.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 53.4% of about 601,000 games and Black 42.2%, with 4.5% drawn. The line is rare under 1400: in 1,891 games White won 57.9% and Black 39.9%. Between 1400 and 1799 White wins 56.8% and Black 40.2%, between 1800 and 2199 it is 54.0% against 41.8%, and from 2200 up 50.3% against 44.1%. In 1,345 master games over the board White won 40.4%, Black 23.7%, and 35.8% were drawn.
