The Fried Liver Attack (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 Nxd5 6.Nxf7) is a line in which White sacrifices a knight on f7 for an attack on Black's king, which is left in the middle of the board. It arises from the [Two Knights Defense](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense) when Black recaptures on d5 with the knight.

### Origins

The Fried Liver has been known for centuries. The earliest known example is a game played by Giulio Cesare Polerio before 1606. It is also called the Fegatello Attack. The name comes from an Italian dish.

### The idea

After 6.Nxf7 Kxf7 7.Qf3+, the king must go to e6 to protect the pinned d5-knight. White then plays 8.Nc3, attacking the pinned knight a second time. Black is a piece up but the king is stuck in the centre. Black usually defends with ...Nb4 and ...c6 to hold the d5-knight.

### Main lines

- 6.Nxf7, the Fried Liver itself: 6...Kxf7 7.Qf3+ Ke6 8.Nc3 Nb4, and ...c6 to reinforce the pinned knight. Here 9.Bb3, 9.Qe4 and 9.O-O are better choices than 9.a3, after which Black is quite strong.
- The [Lolli Attack](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-lolli-attack) 6.d4, which Bobby Fischer considered "incredibly strong". Because of it, 5...Nxd5 is rarely played.

Black can avoid the whole line on move five:

- [5...Na5](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-polerio-defense), the Polerio Defense, generally considered best.
- [5...Nd4](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-fritz-variation), the Fritz Variation, and [5...b5](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-ulvestad-variation), the Ulvestad Variation.
- On move four, [4...Bc5](/openings/italian-game-two-knights-defense-traxler-counterattack), the Traxler Counterattack, which leads to wild complications.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths (for White)**

- A very dangerous attack with simple, natural moves.
- Black's defence is hard to find over the board, especially in fast games.
- It has not been proven to be decisive, but it is extremely difficult to meet in practice.

**Weaknesses (for White)**

- Well-informed opponents never allow it: they play 5...Na5 instead.
- White gives up a whole piece. If the attack is not accurate, Black keeps the extra material.
- After 4.Ng5, White must also know the main lines after 5...Na5, where it is White who is attacked.

### Who should play it

The Fried Liver is a classic weapon for attacking players at club level and in fast games, and a great way to learn attacking chess. With Black, the lesson is simple: after 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5, play 5...Na5 instead of 5...Nxd5.

### The numbers

Below 1400, White wins 71% and Black only 27%. Between 1400 and 1800 it is 69% against 28.5%, and between 1800 and 2200 it is 64% against 32%. Even above 2200 White wins 54% against 42%. There are almost no master games with it: only 63 in the database.
