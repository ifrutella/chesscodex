The Chekhover Variation is 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Qxd4, a line of the [Sicilian Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense) with the code B53. White takes back on d4 with the queen and ignores the usual advice not to develop the queen too early.

### Origins

The variation is named after Vitaly Chekhover, from the game Chekhover–Lisitsin, Leningrad 1938. It is sometimes also called the Szily Variation or the Hungarian Variation. It is somewhat rare at grandmaster level and not uncommon among amateurs.

### Main lines

Black's main reply is 4...Nc6, which attacks the queen at once. White then chooses between three moves:

- 5.Bb5 pins the knight, and after 5...Bd7 6.Bxc6 Bxc6 White has given up the light-squared bishop to keep the queen in the centre. Tal–Byrne, 1976, and Vasiukov–van Wely, 2002, both continued 7.Nc3 Nf6 8.Bg5 e6 9.O-O-O Be7 10.Rhe1 O-O.
- 5.Qa4 avoids the exchange and keeps the light-squared bishop, but it is rated as dubious.
- 5.Qe3 is an unambitious reply, often combined with Be2, O-O, Nc3 (with or without c4) and Bd2. International Master Alexandru-Bogdan Banzea named this set-up the Harikrishna System, after grandmaster Pentala Harikrishna used it over the board with good results as White.

Black has other fourth moves. 4...a6 prevents a later pin, for example 5.c4 Nc6 6.Qd1. 4...Bd7 prepares 5...Nc6, and 4...Nf6 continues development. After 4...Nc6 5.Bb5 the move 5...Qd7 is the [Zaitsev Defense](/openings/sicilian-defense-chekhover-variation-zaitsev-defense).

### Who should play it

- With 4.Qxd4 White stays out of the [Najdorf](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation) and the [Dragon](/openings/sicilian-defense-dragon-variation), which both begin 2...d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4. That makes it a practical choice for players who do not want to study those lines.
- Black players who answer 2.Nf3 with 2...d6 should have a reply ready. The main one is 4...Nc6.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 50.1% of 2.2 million games and Black 44.0%, with 5.9% drawn. Under 1400 Black is ahead, 48.6% against 47.5%, and between 1400 and 1799 White leads narrowly, 48.2% against 47.4%. Between 1800 and 2199 White wins 50.7% and Black 43.5%, and from 2200 up 50.8% against 41.2%. In 6,957 master games over the board White won 34.0%, Black 27.9%, and 38.1% were drawn.
