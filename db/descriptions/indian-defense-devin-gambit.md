The Devin Gambit is 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.g4, an offbeat line in which White offers the g-pawn to the knight on f6. In return White hopes to take control of the centre with a pawn on e4. The code is E00.

### Origins

The opening was played in 1966 at the Lansing Chess Club, where Bill Devin, with White, used it in a game that ended in mate on move 21. By August 1971 it was known in Michigan as the Devin Gambit, and a reader of *Chess Life & Review* asked the grandmaster and columnist Larry Evans whether it simply gave away a pawn. Evans judged it unfavourable for White: in his view, taking the pawn with the knight would not open the position to White's benefit and would not give White enough attacking chances.

The gambit is rare in high-level games, so observers were shocked when Shakhriyar Mamedyarov played it against Andrey Esipenko in a classical game at the 2022 Tata Steel Masters in Wijk aan Zee. Since then it has been seen more often in blitz.

### The idea

The gambit is tricky and may give White practical chances in fast games, but Black has several third moves that lead to comfortable play.

### Main lines

- 3...Bb4+ is the easiest answer according to the Dutch grandmaster Benjamin Bok. Against 4.Nd2 he takes on g4 and meets 5.e4 with 5...f5, arguing that White lacks compensation; against 4.Nc3 he plays 4...d5 with a slight advantage. After 4.Bd2 Bxd2+ 5.Qxd2 Nxg4 6.Nf3 f5 Black has equalised, and after 5.Nxd2 Nxg4 6.e4 f5 7.exf5 exf5 8.Qe2+ Qe7 White is simply a clean pawn down.
- 3...Nxg4 accepts the gambit. Evans thought it left White too little for the pawn, Eric Schiller in *Unorthodox Chess Openings* (1998) judged it better for Black with accurate play, and John L. Watson and Schiller recommend taking at once in *Taming Wild Chess Openings* (2015). After 4.e4 Qh4 5.Nh3 Bb4+ 6.Bd2 Bxd2+ 7.Qxd2 Nf6 Schiller gives Black a significant advantage. With 5.Qe2 Bb4+ 6.Bd2 Nc6 7.Nf3 Bxd2+ 8.Nbxd2 Qh6 White's pieces are active, though it is not clear that this is enough for the pawn. 4.Nf3 d5 gives Black a solid position.
- 3...d5 is another good reply according to Watson and Schiller: after 4.g5 Ne4 Black stands well. Esipenko chose it against Mamedyarov.

### Strengths and weaknesses

- The gambit is a surprise at move three and can unsettle an opponent in a fast game.
- Black has three good answers, and in the main lines White does not get full value for the pawn.
- The advance g2–g4 also loosens White's own kingside.

### Who should play it

It suits White players who enjoy unbalanced positions in blitz and rapid. Black can choose 3...Bb4+, 3...Nxg4 or 3...d5 according to taste.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.4% of the 21,326 games and Black 44.3%, with 4.3% drawn.
