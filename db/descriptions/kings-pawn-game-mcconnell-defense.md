The McConnell Defense, also called the Greco Defence, is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Qf6: Black defends the e5-pawn with the queen. The opening is named after Gioachino Greco (c. 1600 – c. 1634), and the Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings classifies it as C40. It is one of the weaker ways to guard e5, but it has no clear refutation.

### Origins

Greco himself gave this line against the defence in 1620: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Qf6 3.Bc4 Qg6 4.O-O Qxe4 5.Bxf7+ Ke7 6.Re1 Qf4 7.Rxe5+ Kxf7 8.d4 Qf6 9.Ng5+ Kg6 10.Qd3+ Kh6 11.Nf7#. Taking the bishop at once, 5...Kxf7, loses the queen to 6.Ng5+, and 7...Kd8 runs into 8.Re8#.

Two 19th-century games show White's lead in development:

- Morphy–McConnell, New Orleans 1849: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Qf6 3.Nc3 c6 4.d4 exd4 5.e5 Qg6 6.Bd3 Qxg2 7.Rg1 Qh3 8.Rg3 Qh5 9.Rg5 Qh3 10.Bf1 Qe6 11.Nxd4, and Morphy was better. 6.Qxd4 would also have given White a big lead in development.
- Paulsen–Busch, Düsseldorf 1863: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Qf6 3.Bc4 Nh6 4.O-O Bc5 5.Nc3 c6 6.d4 Bxd4 7.Nxd4 exd4 8.e5 Qg6 9.Qxd4, and again White was ahead in development.

### The idea

The queen protects e5, but it takes f6, the most natural square for the knight on g8. The drawback is that the queen comes out early and can become a target. White's advantage consists mainly of smoother development. In the Busch game Black put the knight on h6, so that after d4 and Bxh6 the queen could recapture.

### Main lines

- 3.Bc4 develops with an eye on f7. Greco's line shows the danger of grabbing the e-pawn with ...Qg6 and ...Qxe4.
- 3.Nc3 c6 4.d4 exd4 5.e5 drives the queen away with gain of time, as in the Morphy game.
- After 3.Bc4 Nh6 4.O-O Bc5 5.Nc3 c6, White opened the position with 6.d4 in the Paulsen game.

### Strengths and weaknesses

- It is simple to play and avoids the theory of 2...Nc6 and the [Petrov Defense](/openings/petrovs-defense).
- The queen on f6 can be chased with tempo by Nc3–d5, e4–e5 or Bg5.
- White's edge comes from development, so Black has to catch up quickly.

### Who should play it

It is a popular choice among novice players, and International Master Gary Lane has noted that players who "should know better" have used it too. White does best with natural development and a timely d2–d4 or e4–e5 to gain time on the queen.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 53.9% of 4.6 million games and Black 42.1%, with 4.0% drawn. Under 1400 and between 1400 and 1799 White wins 51.4% in both bands, and between 1800 and 2199 51.8%. From 2200 up, in 8,130 games, White wins 55.5% and Black 39.4%. There are no master games with 2...Qf6 in the database.
