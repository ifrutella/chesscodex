The Barry Attack is a system for White that usually arises from 1.d4 Nf6 2.Nf3 g6 3.Nc3 d5 4.Bf4. It uses the same set-up for White as the [Jobava London](/openings/rapport-jobava-system), here against a kingside fianchetto. It is a Queen's Pawn Opening; the Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings puts it under D00, and on this site the line is filed under A48.

### Practitioners

White normally uses it against the King's Indian, the [East Indian](/openings/east-indian-defense) and the [Pirc](/openings/pirc-defense). Grandmasters Niaz Murshed and Mark Hebden are among its regular practitioners, and so is Aaron Summerscale, who covered it, together with the related 150 Attack, in his book *A Killer Chess Opening Repertoire*. The value of the system has been analysed and debated at length by its supporters and its critics.

### The idea

The Barry Attack usually means a sharp kingside attack by White, often with the sacrifice of one or more pieces to set up a mate. In Blatny–Fette, Vienna 1991, Grandmaster Pavel Blatny drove Black's king into the middle of the board and had a mate in one when Black resigned: 1.d4 Nf6 2.Nf3 g6 3.Nc3 d5 4.Bf4 Bg7 5.e3 Bg4 6.Be2 c6 7.Ne5 Bxe2 8.Qxe2 Nbd7 9.O-O-O O-O 10.h4 Qa5 11.h5 Nxe5 12.dxe5 Ne4 13.hxg6 Nxc3 14.Qh5 Nxa2+ 15.Kb1 fxg6 16.Qxh7+ Kf7 17.Bh6 Rg8 18.Rh4 g5 19.Bxg7 gxh4 20.Bf6+ Ke6 21.Qxe7+ Kf5 22.Qh7+ Ke6 23.e4, 1–0.

### Main lines

- [4...Bg7](/openings/queens-pawn-game-barry-attack-2) is Black's usual reply. The most popular continuation is 5.e3 O-O 6.Be2 ([Grünfeld Variation](/openings/queens-pawn-game-barry-attack-grunfeld-variation)).
- 5.Qd2, the [Tarzan Attack](/openings/queens-pawn-game-barry-attack-tarzan-attack), is also playable.

### Strengths and weaknesses

- White's set-up is the same against several defences, so there is little theory to learn.
- The plans are direct: castle long, push the h-pawn and attack the fianchettoed king.
- Black's fianchetto set-up is solid, and an attack that fails can leave White's king exposed on the queenside.

### Who should play it

It suits White players who like attacking chess and want one system against the King's Indian, the East Indian and the Pirc. Black should be ready for an early h4–h5 and keep enough defenders near the king.

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are level: White and Black each win 47.1% of 342,533 games, and 5.7% are drawn. Under 1400 White wins 48.7% and Black 47.3%; between 1400 and 1799 Black is a little ahead at 48.3% against 47.6%, and between 1800 and 2199 too, 47.6% against 46.9%. From 2200 up White wins 47.5% and Black 44.7%. In 2,290 master games White wins 35.9%, Black 26.6%, and 37.4% are drawn.
