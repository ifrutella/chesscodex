The Magnus Smith Trap is a trap in the [Sozin Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-classical-variation-sozin-attack) of the Sicilian: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 Nc6 6.Bc4 g6? 7.Nxc6 bxc6 8.e5. White breaks open the centre before Black has castled, and the bishop on c4 eyes f7. The code is B57.

### Origins

The trap is named after Magnus Smith (1869–1934), three times champion of Canada. The chess historian Edward Winter has called the name a misnomer, although the Sicilian does have a "Magnus Smith Variation", one of the very rare cases of a player's forename and surname being used together in opening names. In the 1910 World Championship, game 7 of Schlechter–Lasker went 8...Ng4 9.e6 f5, and Black eventually drew. In Rosen–Burn, Paris 1900, 8...d5 9.exf6 dxc4 10.Qxd8+ Kxd8 11.Bg5 Be6 12.O-O-O+ Ke8 was played, and Black even won.

### The idea

After 6.Bc4 a common answer is 6...e6, so that White's bishop "bites on granite". 6...g6 falls into the trap: 7.Nxc6 bxc6 8.e5 attacks the knight on f6 and the pawn on d6 at once, and Black is in a bad way.

### Main lines

- 8...dxe5? leads to instant disaster: 9.Bxf7+ Kxf7 10.Qxd8 wins the queen.
- 8...Nh5 9.Qf3! e6 (9...d5 10.Nxd5!) 10.g4 Ng7 11.Ne4 Qa5+ 12.Bd2 Qxe5 13.Bc3 traps Black's queen, according to Bobby Fischer.
- 8...Ng4 is correct according to Fischer. Even so, White was clearly better in Suetin–Makarichev, Moscow 1983, after 9.Bf4 Bh6 10.Bxh6 Nxh6 11.Qd2 Nf5 12.exd6 Nxd6 13.O-O-O Qc7 14.Qf4 O-O 15.Ne4.
- 8...d5 9.exf6 dxc4 10.Qxd8+ Kxd8 11.Bg5 is the other way out, as in Rosen–Burn.

### Who should know it

Sicilian players who meet 6.Bc4 with ...g6 should know the trap; 6...e6 is a common answer. With White, remember 7.Nxc6 and 8.e5 against an early ...g6 in the Sozin.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 66.9% of the 38,400 games and Black 29.2%, with 3.9% drawn.
