Bird's Defence is 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 Nd4, an uncommon reply to the [Ruy Lopez](/openings/ruy-lopez) with the code C61. Black moves the knight a second time, attacks the bishop on b5 and offers an exchange of knights. This site lists it as the Bird Variation.

### Origins

The defence was published in 1843 in Paul Rudolf von Bilguer's Handbuch des Schachspiels and explored by Henry Bird in the late 19th century. Bird played it as Black at least 25 times, with nine wins, thirteen losses and three draws. Siegbert Tarrasch, Boris Spassky and Alexander Khalifman later used it a few times in tournament play. It is still sometimes seen as a surprise weapon, but no strong master since Bird has adopted it regularly. World champion Magnus Carlsen played it as Black against Ivan Šarić at the 2014 Chess Olympiad and lost.

### Main lines

With careful play White is held to gain an advantage.

- 4.Nxd4 exd4 5.O-O Bc5 6.d3 c6 7.Ba4 Ne7 is given as the best play for both sides.
- 4.Nxd4 exd4 5.O-O Ne7 is the [Paulsen Variation](/openings/ruy-lopez-bird-variation-paulsen-variation).
- The same knight move one move later, 3...a6 4.Ba4 Nd4, is [Bird's Defense Deferred](/openings/ruy-lopez-birds-defense-deferred).

### Who should play it

Bird's Defence is a surprise weapon for players who want to leave the main lines of the Ruy Lopez on move three. A White player who knows 4.Nxd4 exd4 5.O-O has little to fear from it.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.6% of 2.9 million games and Black 44.2%, with 4.2% drawn. Under 1400 White wins 49.9% and Black 46.5%, and between 1400 and 1799 it is 50.8% against 45.4%. Between 1800 and 2199 White wins 52.1% and Black 43.3%, and from 2200 up 51.1% against 42.6%. In 1,053 master games over the board White won 40.4%, Black 23.9%, and 35.7% were drawn.
