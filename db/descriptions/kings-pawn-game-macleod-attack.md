The MacLeod Attack, also called the Centre Pawn Opening, is 1.e4 e5 2.c3. White prepares d2–d4 to build a strong pawn centre before developing the king's knight. The code is C20.

### Origins

Ruy López analysed the opening in his book of 1561, but it has never been popular. The Canadian master Nicholas MacLeod played it 17 times in the New York tournament of 1889; apart from that it has seldom appeared in tournament play.

### The idea

2.c3 prepares d4, and play can transpose to other openings, most likely the [Ponziani Opening](/openings/ponziani-opening) (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.c3) or the [Göring Gambit](/openings/scotch-game-goring-gambit) of the Scotch Game. Eric Schiller considers the opening too slow. In his view Black can answer vigorously with 2...d5, which rules out the transpositions and solves Black's opening problems: after 3.exd5 Qxd5 White has no Nc3 to chase the queen with gain of time, because the c-pawn already stands on c3.

### Main lines

- 2...d5 3.Nf3 was MacLeod's choice against Gossip in New York 1889. The game went 3...dxe4 4.Nxe5 Qd5 5.d4 exd3 6.Nxd3 and was equal after move 10, according to Keres. Another try for Black is 4...Bd6 5.Nc4 Be6 6.d4 exd3, and 3...Nc6 transposes to the Ponziani.
- 2...Nf6 3.d4 was seen in Rusakov–Verlinsky, USSR 1947: 3...Nc6 4.Bg5 h6 5.Bh4 g5 6.Bg3 exd4 7.e5 dxc3, and here 8.Nxc3 would have given White the upper hand with more space (Keres). 3...Nxe4 was tried in Morphy–Bottin, Paris 1858.

### Strengths and weaknesses

- White aims for a big centre with c3 and d4, and the opening can lead into Ponziani or Göring Gambit positions that Black may not know well.
- It is slow: c3 does nothing for development and takes the natural square of the queen's knight.
- 2...d5 is an easy way for Black to equalise, since the queen on d5 cannot be hit by Nc3.

### Who should play it

It suits White players who like the Ponziani or the Göring Gambit and want to reach them by another move order. Black does not need to learn much: 2...d5, Schiller's recommendation, is the simplest answer.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.7% of 3.6 million games and Black 47.3%, with 4.0% drawn. The sides stay close below 2200: under 1400 White wins 48.2% and Black 47.6%, between 1800 and 2199 48.1% against 47.7%. From 2200 up Black is ahead, 50.8% against 43.7%. Only 19 master games are in the database, too few to judge.
