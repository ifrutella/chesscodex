The Poisoned Pawn Variation of the [Najdorf](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation) is 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 a6 6.Bg5 e6 7.f4 Qb6. Black's queen eyes the b2-pawn, which is called "poisoned" because taking it can cost time or material. It is the best known of several lines with this name, and its code is B97.

### Origins

David Bronstein, who drew his 1951 World Championship match with Mikhail Botvinnik 12–12, was one of the pioneers of the line. Bobby Fischer later became a proponent and played it with great success. The most famous games are games 7 and 11 of the 1972 World Championship match between Fischer and Spassky. Fischer had Black in both and took the pawn. In game 7 he reached a secure position with a comfortable material advantage but only drew. In game 11 Spassky surprised him with a theoretical novelty, and after Fischer defended poorly Spassky trapped his queen and won: it was Fischer's only loss in the Poisoned Pawn.

Other leading players took the line up later with success, among them the world champions Garry Kasparov, Viswanathan Anand and Anatoly Karpov.

### The idea

7...Qb6 hits the pawn on b2 and the knight on d4. The usual continuation is 8.Qd2 Qxb2, accepting the pawn ([Poisoned Pawn Accepted](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-poisoned-pawn-accepted) with 9.Rb1 Qa3). The line is one of the most important in the Sicilian from the point of view of theory. Computer chess has made it a battleground, with operators trying to "out-book" each other by going deeper and deeper into the different lines, so it is extremely well researched.

### Main lines

- 8.Qd2 Qxb2 is the usual continuation, and Black takes the pawn.
- 8.Nb3 protects the pawn.
- 8.a3 is another viable option, and it sets a deadly trap: 8...Qxb2?? 9.Na4! traps the black queen.

### Strengths and weaknesses

- Black wins a pawn and gets a sharp, double-edged game.
- The queen on b2 or a3 is far from the king and can be trapped, as Spassky showed in 1972.
- Writing in 2010, FM Graham Burgess said current theory suggests the b2-pawn is "not too heavily laced with arsenic", but that it would be suicidal to enter the line without specialist knowledge.

### Who should play it

It suits players on either side who are ready to study deep, forcing theory. Those who would rather avoid it can choose 8.Nb3 with White, or another seventh move with Black.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 48.2% of 172,719 games and White 46.8%, with 5.1% drawn. Most of the games are played above 1800: between 1800 and 2199 Black wins 49.0% and White 46.6%, and from 2200 up the sides are level, 47.2% for White and 46.7% for Black. In 7,723 master games 62.7% are drawn; White wins 21.5% and Black 15.9%.
