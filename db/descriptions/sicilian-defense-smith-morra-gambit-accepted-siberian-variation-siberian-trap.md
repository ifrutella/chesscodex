The Siberian Trap is a trap in the [Smith-Morra Gambit](/openings/sicilian-defense-smith-morra-gambit-accepted) in which White, after a series of natural moves, can lose the queen. The position is reached by 1.e4 c5 2.d4 cxd4 3.c3 dxc3 4.Nxc3 Nc6 5.Nf3 e6 6.Bc4 Qc7 7.O-O Nf6 8.Qe2 Ng4; on this site the line is filed with the order 7.Qe2 Nf6 8.O-O Ng4. The code is B21.

### Origins

The name seems to come from Boris Schipkov of Novosibirsk in Siberia. The trap has occurred at least twice in tournament play, in Kolenbet–Schipkov, Khabarovsk 1987, and in Tesinsky–Magerramov, Budapest 1990, and a third time in Rohit–Szabo, Spain 2001. In Kramadzhian–Schipkov, Novosibirsk 1988, White met the same fate after 9.Bb3.

### The idea

With 8.Qe2 White prepares e4–e5. The move is playable if White is careful on the next move. 8...Ng4 brings the knight close to White's king, and both the knight and the queen on c7 bear on h2.

### The trap

9.h3?? is the decisive mistake, answered by 9...Nd4!. Black threatens 10...Nxf3+ followed by 11...Qh2#, and this wins material; if 10.Nxd4, then 10...Qh2#. 9.Bb3?? fails the same way, and 9.Rd1 Bc5 does not work either.

The recommended defence is 9.Nb5! Qb8, when Black threatens 10...a6 11.Nc3 Nd4!. After 10.h3 h5 11.g3 Nge5 12.Nxe5 Nxe5 13.Bf4 a6 the position is sharp with roughly equal chances.

### Other eighth moves

- 8.Re1 Bc5 gives Black a good game, since White's f2-square is sensitive.
- 8.h3 a6 does not achieve much for White.
- 8.Nb5 Qb8 9.e5 Nxe5 10.Nxe5 Qxe5 11.Re1 gives White some compensation for the sacrificed pawns.

### Who should know it

Smith-Morra players with White should know it before they play Qe2 with the bishop on c4 and Black's queen on c7. With Black, the set-up with ...Nc6, ...e6, ...Qc7 and ...Nf6 gives chances to set the trap.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black wins 60.1% of the 46,625 games and White 36.4%, with 3.4% drawn.
