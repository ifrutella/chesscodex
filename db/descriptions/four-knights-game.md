The Four Knights Game (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Nc3 Nf6) is a classical open game in which both sides develop their knights first. It is solid and symmetrical, and fairly popular with beginners, but it also contains some sharp lines.

### Origins

The Four Knights was a popular member of the open games in the 19th century. By the First World War its popularity had fallen, as players turned to the Ruy Lopez and other openings. In the 1990s it had a renaissance and is again seen in grandmaster repertoires; Nigel Short, Michael Adams and Alexei Shirov are among those who have played its Double Spanish line. It even appeared in game 5 of Deep Blue versus Kasparov in 1996.

### The idea

Both sides follow the principle "knights before bishops". White's usual plans are to pin the c6-knight with Bb5 (the Spanish Four Knights), open the centre with d4 (the Scotch Four Knights) or play Bc4. Many lines lead to quiet positional play with exchanges on an open file.

### Main lines

- [Spanish Four Knights](/openings/four-knights-game-spanish-variation) 4.Bb5: the main line. Black can mirror with [4...Bb4](/openings/four-knights-game-spanish-variation-double-spanish), play [4...Bc5](/openings/four-knights-game-spanish-variation-classical-variation), or choose the Rubinstein Countergambit 4...Nd4.
- [Scotch Four Knights](/openings/four-knights-game-scotch-variation) 4.d4: after [4...exd4](/openings/four-knights-game-scotch-variation-accepted) 5.Nxd4 the main line follows. The Belgrade Gambit 5.Nd5 is a distant second in popularity.
- [Italian Four Knights](/openings/four-knights-game-italian-variation) 4.Bc4: Black can use the "fork trick" 4...Nxe4, a pseudo-sacrifice of the knight that regains the piece after 5.Nxe4 d5.
- [Halloween Gambit](/openings/four-knights-game-halloween-gambit) 4.Nxe5: a dubious sacrifice of a knight for a pawn to build a big centre.
- The Glek System 4.g3, a modern fianchetto approach.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Easy to learn and very solid: natural development with few early traps for White.
- Avoids a lot of the theory of the Ruy Lopez and Petrov.
- It has several sharper options, such as the Scotch Four Knights and the Belgrade Gambit.

**Weaknesses**

- A drawish reputation: Black equalises fairly easily with accurate play.
- The symmetry can make it hard for White to create winning chances.
- Black has active counters such as the Rubinstein Countergambit and the fork trick.

### Who should play it

The Four Knights is ideal for beginners and improving players who want sound, principled development, and for players who want a solid game with White against 1...e5. It is effective against opponents who don't know the specific tricks in the Spanish and Italian lines. It gives the least against well-prepared players who are happy to equalise and hold.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48–49% and Black 45.5–46.5% below 2200, and 48% against 43% above it. In master games over the board 50% of games are drawn, White wins 27% and Black 23%.
