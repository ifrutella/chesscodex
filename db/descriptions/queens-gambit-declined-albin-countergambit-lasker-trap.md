The Lasker Trap is a trap in the [Albin Countergambit](/openings/queens-gambit-declined-albin-countergambit) that arises after 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e5 3.dxe5 d4 4.e3 Bb4+ 5.Bd2 dxe3. If White now takes the bishop, Black's pawn marches on to g1 and promotes to a knight, an underpromotion as early as move seven. The code is D08.

### Origins

The trap is named after Emanuel Lasker, although Serafino Dubois noted it first. Lasker himself reached the position as Black in a consultation game in Moscow in 1899 against Blumenfeld, Boyarkow and Falk.

### The idea

After 3...d4 the black pawn is stronger than it looks. 4.e3 is careless; the usual and better move is 4.Nf3. After 4...Bb4+ 5.Bd2 dxe3 White's best is 6.fxe3, accepting doubled pawns, which the Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings gives as the best move. Black is slightly better, but White has avoided the worst and can defend.

### The trap

6.Bxb4? walks into it: 6...exf2+. Now 7.Kxf2 loses the queen to 7...Qxd1, so White has to play 7.Ke2, and then 7...fxg1=N+ is the point of the trap. Promoting to a queen would be weaker, since 7...fxg1=Q 8.Qxd8+ Kxd8 9.Rxg1 is fine for White. After the knight promotion, 8.Rxg1 Bg4+ skewers White's queen, so the king must move again: 8.Ke1 Qh4+ 9.Kd2 (9.g3 loses the rook on h1 to the fork 9...Qe4+) 9...Nc6, and White is hopelessly lost. After 10.Bc3, 10...Bg4 followed by ...O-O-O+ is crushing.

### The 1899 consultation game

In the Moscow game White answered 5...dxe3 with 6.Qa4+?, and Black wins after this move too. The game went 6...Nc6 7.Bxb4 Qh4 8.Ne2 Qxf2+ 9.Kd1 Bg4 10.Nc3 O-O-O+ 11.Bd6 cxd6 12.e6 fxe6 13.Kc1 Nf6 14.b4 d5 15.b5 Ne5 16.cxd5 Nxd5 17.Qc2 Nb4 18.Nd1+ Nxc2 19.Nxf2 Rd2, and White resigned.

### Who should know it

Every Albin player with Black should know the trap: on Lichess the position after 5...dxe3 has come up in more than 190,000 games. With White, 4.Nf3 avoids it, and after 4.e3 Bb4+ 5.Bd2 dxe3 the right move is 6.fxe3.

### The numbers

On Lichess Black does far better from the position after 5...dxe3: Black wins 64.9% of 192,581 games and White 31.1%, with 4.0% drawn. The figures hardly change with rating, from 65.0% for Black under 1400 and 66.4% between 1400 and 1799 to 63.9% between 1800 and 2199 and 63.1% from 2200 up. Only 2 master games are in the database, too few to judge.
