The Opočenský Variation, also called the Classical Variation of the [Najdorf](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation), is 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 a6 6.Be2. White prepares to castle kingside and goes for a quieter, more positional game. Efim Geller was an early proponent of the move.

### Black's choices

- 6...e5 keeps the game in "pure" Najdorf territory.
- 6...e6 transposes to the [Scheveningen Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-scheveningen-variation).
- 6...Nbd7 is another option.

### Among White's sixth moves

The Najdorf allows White a wide choice: grandmasters have played 22 different replies to 5...a6. 6.Be2 is the quieter alternative to the [English Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-english-attack) 6.Be3 and the old main line 6.Bg5. Other sixth moves include 6.Bc4, the [Fischer–Sozin Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-lipnitsky-attack), 6.f4, the [Amsterdam Variation](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-amsterdam-variation), 6.f3, 6.g3 and 6.h3, the [Adams Attack](/openings/sicilian-defense-najdorf-variation-adams-attack).

### The numbers

On Lichess the results are level: Black wins 47.6% of the 2.2 million games and White 47.0%. Masters draw almost half the time: in 16,301 games over the board 46.1% were drawn, White won 28.7% and Black 25.3%.
