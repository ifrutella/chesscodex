The Modern Defense (1.e4 g6) is a flexible hypermodern reply to 1.e4. Black fianchettoes the king's bishop at once and lets White build the "ideal" centre with pawns on d4 and e4, planning to attack and undermine it later instead of occupying it.

### Origins

The opening is also known as the Robatsch Defense, after Karl Robatsch. In 1965 the tenth edition of *Modern Chess Openings* grouped it with the Pirc as the "Pirc–Robatsch Defense". Raymond Keene and George Botterill published *The Modern Defence* in 1972, and the opening had a wave of popularity in the 1970s. The Canadian grandmaster Duncan Suttles was one of its leading exponents, and the British grandmasters Nigel Davies and Colin McNab have played it. Magnus Carlsen calls a variant with ...Nf6 and ...Nh5 the "Norwegian Rat", and he played it against Michael Adams at the 2010 Olympiad.

### The idea

Black plays ...g6 and ...Bg7 and delays developing the knight to f6. That is the main difference from the closely related [Pirc Defense](/openings/pirc-defense), and there are many ways to transpose between the two. Because the e4-pawn is not attacked yet, White has the option of blunting the g7-bishop with c2–c3, or of playing c4, as in the Averbakh System.

### Main lines

- [Standard line](/openings/modern-defense-standard-defense) 2.d4 Bg7 3.Nc3 d6, with continuations such as [4.f4](/openings/modern-defense-pseudo-austrian-attack) (for example 4...c6 5.Nf3 Bg4) and [4.Nf3](/openings/modern-defense-two-knights-variation).
- [Averbakh System](/openings/modern-defense-averbakh-system) with c4: 4...Nf6 there leads to a position of the King's Indian Defense.
- [Three Pawns Attack](/openings/modern-defense-three-pawns-attack) 3.f4.
- The "Monkey's Bum", an early Bc4 and Qf3 line discovered by IM Nigel Povah in the 1970s, and the Norwegian Defense 2.d4 Nf6, with 3.e5 Nh5 as one of its lines.

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

- Flexible and tough, with transpositions to the Pirc, the King's Indian and the Sicilian.

**Weaknesses**

- White is allowed the "ideal" centre with pawns on d4 and e4.
- Delaying ...Nf6 gives White extra options such as c2–c3 and the lines with c4.
- It has provoked some very aggressive replies, such as the Monkey's Bum.

### Who should play it

The Modern suits players who are happy to let White build the centre and attack it later, and who like to keep their options open.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 48.6–49.1% and Black 46.2–47.4% in every band below 2200, and 47.3% against 46.2% above it. In master games over the board White wins 36.4%, Black 30.8%, and 32.8% are drawn.
