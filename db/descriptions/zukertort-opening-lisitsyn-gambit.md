The Lisitsin Gambit (Lisitsyn in the Lichess spelling) is 1.Nf3 f5 2.e4: against the [Dutch-style 1...f5](/openings/zukertort-opening-dutch-variation), White offers the e-pawn at once. The Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings registers it under A04. *The Oxford Companion to Chess* calls the gambit "enterprising and probably sound".

### Origins

The gambit became widely known in 1931, when Georgy Lisitsin played it, probably for the first time in an official game, against Nikolai Riumin in the first round of the USSR Championship in Moscow. Lisitsin used it again against Mikhail Botvinnik at the Leningrad Masters tournament of 1933.

### The idea

2.e4 looks counter-intuitive: after 2...fxe4 White cannot make a developing move and has to move the knight a second time. In practice, though, the knight on g5 is hard to drive away and creates tactical threats, more than just Nxe4, that make up for the pawn. The gambit lines are also easy to learn alongside the lines of the [Dutch Defence](/openings/dutch-defense), since play can transpose to it.

White can also delay the gambit by one move with 2.d3, wait for Black's reply (often 2...d6) and then play 3.e4. Some authors call this the Improved Lisitsin Gambit.

### Main lines

- 2...fxe4 is the only way to challenge White's idea. After 3.Ng5 play can go 3...d5 4.d3 Qd6 5.Nc3 h6. If Black clings to the extra pawn with 4...exd3, White can strike with 5.Bxd3 Nf6 6.Bxh7 Nxh7 7.Qh5+ Kd7 8.Nf7, and 8...Qe8? loses to 9.Qxd5#.
- 3...Nf6 4.d3 e3 5.Bxe3 e5 gives the pawn back. 4...d5 5.dxe4 h6 6.Nf3 and 4...e5 5.dxe4 Bc5 6.Bc4 are the other options.
- 3...e5 4.d3 e3 5.Bxe3 Nc6 is a line given by Cvetkovic.
- 2...e5 transposes to the [Latvian Gambit](/openings/latvian-gambit). For a Black player who knows nothing about the Lisitsin this may be a good practical choice, since it gives Black more of the initiative.

### Strengths and weaknesses

- White answers 1...f5 with a gambit on move two instead of a quiet set-up.
- The knight on g5 and the threat of Nxe4 give White active play for the pawn.
- Black can return the pawn with ...e3 and reach a normal position, or answer 2...e5 and take the game into the Latvian.

### Who should play it

It suits 1.Nf3 players who want an active answer to the Dutch. Black players who meet 1.Nf3 with 1...f5 should know one line after 2...fxe4 3.Ng5, for example the early ...e3 that returns the pawn.

### The numbers

On Lichess White wins 51.0% of 222,162 games and Black 45.3%, with 3.7% drawn. Below 1800 the sides are close: 48.9% for White in both bands, against 47.6% for Black under 1400 and 48.3% between 1400 and 1799. Between 1800 and 2199 White wins 52.3% and Black 44.0%, and from 2200 up White wins 49.5% and Black 44.2%. In 331 master games White wins 37.2%, Black 33.8%, and 29.0% are drawn.
